Introduction

I follow this Code of Conduct since the beginning of my charting adventure in 2020.
The only problem with that is that I've been navigating in a gray area this whole time and I've always been aware of the potential damage I may cause with it. It's not my intention to do harm to others, so I immediately put rules in place.

My goal is to follow fair use rules in the best way possible while nullifying the negative effects of this project with the positives.
Every single thing from Project R is made without intention of copyright infringement of any kind, and all credits are given to the right people.

Project R Code of Conduct

Let's talk about the six golden rules I follow for Project R.
Those will probably sound like an excuse for everything I'm doing, but I feel it's best for me to be 100% transparent with this.

1. I'm not earning money from this.

The most important thing. Following fair use means using copyrighted content for educational purposes and, therefore, giving up on making money. Basically, I'm not earning money off of downloads, plays or anything else related to Project R. I don't do commissions either, but I'm happy to get requests.

2. I'm using the track only for charting.

The audio I get will only be used in charting sessions and will be distributed in a lossy format, specifically .opus.
I'm not allowed to share lossless audio nor I'd like to do it because charts would be too big.

3. I'm not charting new songs.

Songs that have been released in the last 12 months will not be charted if they can generate revenue.
It sounds stupid, but for me it feels wrong (and risky) to chart a song that was recently released.

For example, if something came out in April 2026, I won't begin charting it until April 2027.

4. I'm always giving full credits for the song.

The artist is always credited in the best way possible, with links to the song given whenever possible.
I did nothing but create a chart for a rhythm game. They deserve recognition for their work.

5. I'm not responsible for misusage of the downloaded content.

I have no idea what the people will do with the things they download and I'm not responsible for it. This stuff is intended to be used only on rhythm games and nowhere else, and it will always be like this.
Charts are shared as an .sng file that contains the song masked with a simple algorithm, basically unreadable without dedicated programs, and unpackaging of said file is not allowed.
*older charts are still in a RAR archive but I'm working on changing that

6. Artists have the last word on everything.

While navigating in this gray area, I'm always trying to respect the work of others while not bothering you with my little things.
If I missed something (for example, you want a link or a copyright notice inside the chart), you can tell me and I'll fix it.
And even after everything I said, if you want a chart removed with one of your songs... it sucks, but I'll do it without complaining. It's your stuff, after all. I can't guarantee it'll disappear, but I'll do my best to stop it from being shared.
